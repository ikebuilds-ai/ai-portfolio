# Rubik Resilience — Suburb-Level Disaster-Risk Intelligence That Shows Its Work

**Disaster-risk scoring at a community level** built on an **evidence ladder** — every risk conclusion is backed by sources a human can climb, not a black box that says "trust the model."

## What it is

- **Suburb-level disaster-risk scoring** — assesses resilience at a community/street level, the scale where evacuation and insurance decisions actually get made.
- **Evidence ladder** — every claim is traceable to a source (drawn from government data), so a reader can climb from the headline score down to the exact data behind each link in the chain.
- **Clear triage, not a vague number** — output is an actionable **MONITOR / INVESTIGATE / ESCALATE** signal, so a decision-maker knows what it means and what to do next.
- **Built for real use** — engineered with a council-facing shadow pilot in mind (disaster intelligence), with a public brand site at **rubikresilience.com**. It was developed from the Gold Coast Innovation Hub **Open Data Challenge (disaster resilience)**, where it was **recognised and pitched live on stage** (hosts never publicly announced formal rankings, so this is described honestly as *recognised*, not "winner").

## Engineering depth

- **Multi-source grounding** — pulls from government/open disaster and community data and structures it into a coherent risk view, rather than one model's opinion.
- **Auditable reasoning** — the evidence ladder turns an AI conclusion into something a human can inspect, challenge, and re-check. Each claim carries its source.
- **Decision-gated output** — the system recommends an action (monitor / investigate / escalate), it does not make the decision. A person reads the sources and decides.
- **Honest uncertainty** — where evidence is thin, the ladder makes that visible instead of hiding it behind a confident number.

## The idea

**Data Presents. Human Decides.** Rubik presents every risk score with an evidence ladder a person can climb; a human reads it and decides what to do. No black boxes — especially not when lives and property are on the line.
